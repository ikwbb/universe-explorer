# Data sources and representation

- Spacecraft: NASA/JPL Horizons Vector Tables, https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/ and https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/horizons.html . Each public/data/<mission>.json records the request URL, original header, retrieval date, frame, units and state vectors. Data downloaded 13 September 2026. Predictions have limited coverage, notably Psyche and Hayabusa2.
- Coordinate frame: heliocentric ecliptic J2000, km and km/s. Rendering maps (x,y,z) to (x,z,-y). Julian TDB epochs are treated as UTC in the educational clock, introducing roughly a minute of time-scale offset.
- Interpolation: cubic Hermite between authoritative samples. Coarse cruise samples are supplemented with denser encounter, final-mission and recent-orbit intervals. This is not a complete SPICE reconstruction. Paths connect sampled vectors; predictions are dashed. Historical ephemerides are reconstructed positions, not raw observations.
- Planets: rounded J2000 elements and physical properties from JPL SSD / NASA. https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/approx_pos.html and https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/ . The implementation uses fixed two-body elements, not the full secular-rate model or N-body dynamics. The Earth approximation represents the Earth–Moon system to educational precision.
- Moons: approximate semimajor axes, periods, inclinations and radii; phases and orientations are representative. Binary-star orbits and proper motion are not simulated.
- Nearby stars: rounded J2000 sky coordinates and representative distances / properties from SIMBAD and nearby-star literature. https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/ . Planet confirmation status is deliberately qualified where not maintained.
- Galaxies: approximate sky directions and distances, NASA Hubble catalogues and SIMBAD. https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/explore-the-night-sky/ . Galaxy morphology, orientation, dust, stellar density and individual particles are illustrative. The catalogue is representative, not complete.
- Earth and Moon textures: official Three.js example assets, https://threejs.org/examples/textures/planets/earth_atmos_2048.jpg and https://threejs.org/examples/textures/planets/moon_1024.jpg . Earth imagery derives from NASA Blue Marble-style imagery distributed with the examples.

True size uses physical body radii. Visible size enlarges small objects; spacecraft markers have a separate exaggeration toggle. Physical positions and measurement distances are never altered for layout. Heliosphere / Oort Cloud boundaries are approximate. Radio animations take eight seconds per leg; timestamps use distance / c with frozen endpoints and an immediate hypothetical reply. No actual command is sent.

Known simplifications: star radii are rounded; no complete moon mass catalogue; mission status is descriptive rather than a live operations feed; no atmospheric scattering model, detailed spacecraft meshes or precise gravity-assist reconstruction. All mission metadata should be rechecked against NASA/ESA/JAXA before scientific reuse.
